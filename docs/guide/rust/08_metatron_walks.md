# Quantum walks on Metatron's Cube

`metatron-qso-rs/examples/metatron_walks.rs` analyses two 13-node graphs:
Metatron's Cube (all 78 lines between its 13 circles, the complete graph
K₁₃) and the Fruit of Life (circles that touch). Node 0 is the centre,
nodes 1–6 the inner ring and 7–12 the outer ring.

## Geometry and symmetry

`automorphism_group` computes the symmetries of a graph: the node
permutations that preserve its edges. Its orbits are the classes of
equivalent nodes; the Fruit of Life has the 12 symmetries of a hexagon,
Metatron's Cube all 13! permutations.
`line_lengths` lists the distinct lengths of the lines of Metatron's Cube
with their multiplicities.

{{example metatron_walks.rs:geometry}}

## The coherent walk from the centre

A continuous-time walk generated by the Laplacian, started at the centre,
never settles, but its time average does: `limiting_distribution` is the
long-time average of the probabilities, computed from the eigenspaces.
On K₁₃ the walk mostly stays where it started: the time-averaged
probability of the centre is 145/169. The mixing time (Aharonov,
Ambainis, Kempe and Vazirani) is the first time after which the
time-averaged distribution stays within total-variation distance ε of its
limit.

{{example metatron_walks.rs:coherent}}

## Dephasing

With dephasing in the node basis (a Lindblad equation), the walk loses
its coherence and behaves like a classical random walk, which converges
to the uniform distribution. The program prints the total-variation
distance to uniform at t = 20 for growing dephasing rates.

{{example metatron_walks.rs:dephasing}}

## Spatial search

Childs–Goldstone search adds a marked-node term to γ times the
Laplacian. `critical_gamma` is their critical rate (1/N)·Σ 1/λ over the
non-zero Laplacian eigenvalues, which the best rate approaches on large
vertex-transitive graphs (on K₁₃ it is 12/169, the best rate 1/13);
`SpatialSearch::optimize` searches γ and the time for
the highest success probability of finding outer node 9, which the
program compares with the mean classical hitting time of a random walk.
